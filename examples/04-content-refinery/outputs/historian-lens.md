# Historian lens (analysis) — completed

> Analysis (parallel): where does this method come from and how did it evolve?

SUMMARY: The consultants debated three possible foundations for the origin story — documentary evidence, material artifacts, and oral tradition. One argued for a strict documentary reconstruction, another for a material‑culture focus, and a third advocated an integrated synthesis that cross‑references all three sources to build a cohesive timeline.

DECISION: We will adopt an integrated synthesis approach that weaves together documentary records, material artifacts, and oral histories to create a unified origin narrative.

RATIONALE: The integrated synthesis best captures the full evolution of the subject by leveraging the strengths of each evidence type, ensuring coherence, testability, and a richer interpretive framework. This argument was the most persuasive because it balances completeness with analytical rigor.

REJECTED: 
- Pure documentary reconstruction – rejected for ignoring material and oral evidence.  
- Pure material‑culture reconstruction – rejected for overlooking textual context and narrative continuity.  

RISKS: Accepted risk is the potential exclusion of marginalized or poorly documented perspectives that may not survive in the surviving record.

ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA: The deliverable is correct when it presents a coherent, chronologically ordered narrative that explicitly references at least three distinct categories of evidence (documentary, material, oral) and clearly explains each category’s contribution to the origin story.
