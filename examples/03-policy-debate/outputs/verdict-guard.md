**4‑DAY WORK WEEK PILOT RECOMMENDATION**

Recommendation: Adopt a 3‑month, company‑wide pilot of a 4‑day (32‑hour) work week at the same salary for volunteer teams, with the option to expand after evaluation.

Rationale: The pilot allows the company to test whether reduced hours can maintain client reliability while preserving economic viability, providing empirical data before any broader rollout. A time‑boxed pilot is preferable to immediate company‑wide change or maintaining the status‑quo because it limits risk and enables informed decision‑making based on measured outcomes.

Acceptance Criteria:
1. Delivery integrity: no missed committed delivery dates or SLA violations attributable to the schedule change.
2. Financial neutrality: billable hours per employee have not declined by more than 10 % compared to baseline, or any decline is offset by measurable revenue gains or cost savings.
3. Productivity stability: average story points or feature velocity per team within ±5 % of baseline, and defect rate has not increased.
4. Employee well‑being: employee satisfaction (eNPS or survey score) shows a statistically significant improvement or at least no degradation.
5. Client satisfaction: client‑feedback scores (NPS, post‑delivery surveys) remain unchanged or improve.

Risk Mitigations:
- SLA breach risk: teams must preserve output quality and client commitments; velocity and defect rates must stay within agreed thresholds.
- Financial impact: ≤10 % billable‑hour tolerance; track revenue/cost offsets; adjust or halt pilot if exceeded.
- Uneven adoption / morale: limit pilot to volunteer teams; monitor fairness with satisfaction surveys.
- Measurement noise: use 3‑month horizon; aggregate multiple metrics (velocity, defect rate, output, well‑being, client satisfaction).
